## Design Context

### Users
Broad audience: fellow developers, potential freelance clients, friends, and the wider tech community. No single dominant persona — the site needs to impress technical peers while remaining approachable to non-technical visitors. Every visitor should leave thinking "this person is seriously talented, fun to work with, and I want to hire them."

### Brand Personality
**Sharp, restless, crafted.** David has maintained this site for 6+ years — it's not a weekend project, it's a living artifact of someone who genuinely cares about the craft. The personality should read as a developer who builds things because they can't stop, not because they're padding a resume. Confident without being arrogant. Technical depth presented with visual clarity.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Boldness: 6-7/10** — noticeably striking and opinionated, but still functional and navigable. Not an art experiment, but not a template either. Think: a portfolio that makes other developers want to redesign theirs.
- **Dark mode is the primary experience.** Light mode exists and should be genuinely designed (not an afterthought), but dark is home.
- **Open to a palette shift.** The current teal-to-blue gradient has served its time. Bolder, more distinctive color choices are welcome — the palette should feel ownable, not interchangeable with any other dev portfolio.
- **Typography should evolve.** Graphik has been the workhorse — consider a more distinctive pairing that signals personality beyond "geometric sans."
- **Motion should be purposeful and polished.** The site already has scroll reveals and animations — these should feel choreographed, not decorative.

### Design Principles
1. **Craft over convention.** Every detail should look intentional. If it looks like a default, redesign it.
2. **Dark-first, light-considered.** Dark mode gets the full creative treatment. Light mode should feel like a sibling, not a palette swap.
3. **Show, don't tell.** The site itself is the portfolio piece. The quality of the interface should demonstrate the talent before any project card is clicked.
4. **Bold but navigable.** Visual boldness should never come at the cost of usability. Striking layouts, clear hierarchy, fast interactions.
5. **Evolve the identity.** After 6 years, honor the DNA (clean, modern, technical) while pushing it somewhere new. The next version should feel like a step forward, not a lateral move.
